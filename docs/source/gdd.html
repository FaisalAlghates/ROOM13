Game Design Document (GDD)

Game Name:
ROOM 13
Genre:
First-person psychological horror · stealth & survival. Single-player, story-driven, about 7 minutes of linear play with a built-in checkpoint loop.
Game Elements:The basic activities the player does for fun
  • Explore a decaying hospital at 03:17 AM, in the dark, one corridor at a time.
  • Manage light: a flashlight with a draining battery, spare batteries, and a darkness the Patient prefers.
  • Stay unseen: sneak, hide in lockers with the light off, throw a pipe to pull the Patient away.
  • Hold him back with light: the Patient cannot approach while he is lit; he only moves when not observed.
  • Solve the route: keycards, locked doors, CCTV monitors, documents and a phone/map.
  • Run: sprint, slide under low obstacles in the final chase, reach the exit.
Player:Number of players at once
1 (single-player, offline).

Technical Specs

Technical Form:2D (flat) or 3D (form)
Real-time 3D. Unity 6 High Definition Render Pipeline: volumetric fog, per-light shadows, bloom/grain/chromatic aberration. All geometry, props, textures and audio are generated procedurally by code at start-up - no imported art or audio files.
View:Camera view for the player
First person. Eye height 1.62 m standing / 0.95 m crouched; head-bob, camera roll, shake, and a controlled fear camera in scripted moments.
Platform:
PC - Windows 10/11 64-bit, DirectX 12 GPU (Mono scripting backend). Distributed as a zip with launcher for itch.io.
Language:Programming
C# (Unity 6000.x, Mono). Interface and story texts in English and Arabic (right-to-left shaping implemented in code).
Device:
PC (keyboard + mouse). Mobile and console are not targeted.
One-line pitch. You are Patient 013. The hospital has twelve rooms and a thirteenth door that should not exist. Something tall and silent only moves when you stop looking at it - so you keep the light on him and run.

Game Play

You open your eyes on the cold tiles of the Recovery ward. The phone in your hand says 3:17 AM - No signal. A fluorescent tube stutters overhead and paints the room red every few seconds. Beside you lie scattered papers, a battered flashlight and a plastic access card with only a number embossed so hard it almost cut through: 13.

You thumb the flashlight on and the beam shows every flaw in the corridor: water-stained walls, a wheelchair left mid-turn, doors with narrow wired-glass windows. A yellow diamond on your HUD says the first objective is 57 m away. In the security room, three CRT monitors show corridors you have not walked yet - and on one of them, thirty seconds from now, you are already standing there. Somewhere in the west wing a door opens by itself.

The Patient arrives without footsteps: a tall black silhouette with burning eyes and arms too long for his body. If you look at him he slows to a crawl; if you turn away he is closer. Your only rule is the one written in a child's handwriting on the Records room wall: keep the light on him. Back away with the beam on his face through the side door, sprint west with your lungs burning, curl into a laundry locker with the light off and your hand around a loose pipe while his breath moves along the metal door - then throw the pipe and slip out the other way.

The deeper you go, the less the building agrees with itself. The Patient file in the Records room lists your photograph, your admission time, and a note that you remember every attempt. Knocking comes three times from a door nobody is behind. And when you finally walk back down the main corridor, there is a new door next to the room where you woke up: ROOM 13.

Game Play Outline

1. Opening the game application

Double-click ROOM13_Launcher.exe (Play Fullscreen / Play Window / Watch Auto-Play) or run Game\ROOM13.exe, which opens fullscreen. The first screen is the main menu: a slow camera moves through a fogged hospital corridor behind the title ROOM 13 - 03:17 AM and the line "Headphones recommended. Play in the dark."

2. Game options

  • Language - L switches English / Arabic live (menu, HUD, hints, documents, credits).
  • Pause - ESC: Resume · Language · Quit to desktop.
  • Display - F11 toggles fullscreen / window. Start-up flag -windowed is used by the launcher's Window mode.
  • Hint - H shows the current objective text; a persistent HUD marker also shows the direction and distance.

3. Story synopsis

It is 03:17 AM. Patient 013 wakes on the floor of a hospital whose maintenance logs say "The corridor has twelve rooms. The floor plan has twelve rooms. The fire map has twelve rooms. Stop filing reports about a thirteenth door." A tall thing the staff call the Patient does not tolerate light and is only dangerous when unobserved. Previous patients are listed as "Did not wake", "Missing" and "Refused to turn off his torch. Battery failed at 03:52." Patient 013 is different: he remembers every attempt. He must collect a keycard, read his own file, survive the Patient in three set-piece encounters, and find the way out - even though the room that matters most is Room 13 itself.

8. Winning

Reach the exit door at the end of the maintenance tunnels while the Patient is hunting you, and step out into the street. The game then rolls the credits and ends with "No patients were discharged."

9. Losing

You lose when the Patient gets within about 1.15 m of you while he is able to kill. The screen cuts to a short death state and you restart from the last checkpoint (position, items, battery and spares are restored) with an attempt counter that the ending acknowledges. There are no health bars: one mistake is fatal, and that is what makes every decision count.

10. End

The street outside glows with red and blue light; the phone buzzes: "ROOM 13 - OCCUPIED". The last caption says "Not the room." The player escaped the building, but the credits make clear the story did not end. The game returns to the main menu.

11. Why is all this fun?

  • Light is the weapon and the clock. Every second the beam is on the Patient is a second you are safe - and a second of battery you will not get back.
  • Rules you can learn, not scripts you can memorise. The documents teach the rules one scrap at a time, then each encounter asks you to use them under pressure.
  • A short, intense arc. Eleven stages, no filler: scare → relief → clue → chase → hide → reveal → run.
  • Bilingual and personal. Full Arabic support with correct text shaping; the story is built around the player's own repeated attempts.

Key Features

List of game elements attractive to the player.

  • The Patient AI - a state machine (Patrol, Investigate, Search, Chase, Hunt, Leaving) that reacts to light, noise, line of sight and being watched.
  • Light as a mechanic - lit/observed rules, battery, flicker, spare cells.
  • Stealth & noise model - sprint, walk, crouch, doors, throws and the flashlight all emit noise events.
  • Hiding & distraction - lockers and a throwable pipe.
  • 100 % procedural content - level geometry, textures, props and audio are generated in code; the full game is a ~58 MB download.
  • Live-synthesised audio - breaths, heartbeat, rusty iron doors, drone, chase music; reacts to tension.
  • English / Arabic with right-to-left text shaping.
  • Always-visible guidance - objective text, distance marker and edge arrows so the player never loses the route.

Design Document

This section describes how the game objects behave, how they are controlled and what their properties are - the "mechanics". It is modular: further documents (levels, audio, narrative) can be attached to the concept document.

Design GuidelinesCreative restrictions and the overall goal of the design.

  • Goal: a tight, scary 10-minute experience a stranger can finish in one sitting.
  • No combat. The player cannot hurt the Patient; light only holds him back.
  • Darkness is the art direction. Low-key lighting, red emergency light as the danger colour, teal fog as the safe colour.
  • Never lost, never safe. The route is always indicated; the threat is the only thing that is uncertain.
  • Small footprint. Everything generated at runtime, so the build is small and easy to share.
  • Accessible language. English and Arabic are equal citizens in the UI.

Game Design DefinitionsHow a player wins, loses, transitions between levels, and the main focus of gameplay.

WinLeave the building through the exit door (stage 11).
LoseCaught by the Patient (distance < ~1.15 m while he can kill) → death state → restart from checkpoint.
TransitionStory triggers (entering a zone, picking an item, finishing a hide) advance the stage; checkpoints store position, yaw, items, battery and spares.
Main focusManaging light and attention: when to look, when to run, when to hide.

Game Design Definitions - Rules

Light & observation

RuleBehaviour
LitIf the Patient is inside the flashlight cone he cannot approach and may flinch or retreat.
ObservedIf the player is looking at him (line of sight inside the view cone) he is slowed to 1.3 m/s. Look away and he moves at his state speed.
Battery100 % → 0 at 0.33 %/s. At low charge the flashlight flickers; at 0 the light dies (the HUD "LIGHT" text turns red at ~10 %). A spare cell (R) restores it; the HUD shows "+N" spares.
Beam strengthSpot light with ~520 lumens base intensity, with random and scripted flicker.

Stealth & noise

SourceNoise radius
Sprint step16 m
Walk step4.5 m
Crouch step0 (silent)
Door, thrown object, object impact, flashlight toggle, scriptedEmit noise events of their own kind that the Patient can investigate.

Movement & stamina

PropertyValue
Walk / Sprint / Crouch2.1 / 5.7 / 1.1 m/s
Stamina7.5 s of sprint. Regenerates at 1.3 /s standing still and 0.75 /s while moving. At 0 the player is locked out of sprinting until stamina recovers to 35 %; a panting breath plays.
Collider height1.75 m standing, 1.05 m crouched. Crouching under low pipes is required in the tunnels.
Slide (final stage)While sprinting, press C or Ctrl: a 0.9 s burst (1.45× sprint speed fading to 0.5×) with the body lowered; the player cannot stand while the slide lasts, and stays crouched afterwards if there is a low obstacle overhead. 0.7 s cooldown.

Hiding & doors

  • Locker: E to enter/leave. The player must switch the light off (F) and stay quiet; the Patient may search lockers he saw the player enter.
  • Doors: open, creak, can be slammed and can be broken by the Patient. A locked door beeps (deny) and shows a message; with the right keycard it beeps (ok) and unlocks.

The Patient (Enemy Definition)

"A tall black figure with burning eyes. Very long arms. Head bent sideways. HE ONLY MOVES WHEN YOU DONT LOOK. KEEP THE LIGHT ON HIM." - child's drawing in the Records room.

He is a single AI agent that uses a navigation graph of the hospital and a perception model: sight range 13 m, line of sight, noise events, and "is lit / is observed" flags. He opens doors (and waits at locked ones), checks lockers he saw the player enter, and breathes in 3D so the player can hear him before seeing him.

State machine

StateSpeedBehaviour
Inactive-Not in the world (story pauses him).
ScriptedcinematicUnder the story director's control (jump-scares, monitors).
Patrol1.25 m/sWalks through patrol areas; looks around.
Investigate2.1 m/sHeard a noise: goes to the source and stops.
Search2.1 m/sLost the player: searches near the last known position and checks lockers.
Chase5.3 m/sSees the player. Slower than the player's sprint (5.7 m/s) so a clean run can outpace him.
Hunt6.1 m/sAlways knows where the player is (final chase). Faster than sprint - the player must use the slide and the route.
Leaving-Withdraws when lit or when the scene ends.

Observed overrides speed to 1.3 m/s while the player looks at him. Lit prevents approach. Kill distance 1.15 m.

User Interface (UI)

Keyboard + mouse. Keys are grouped by hand position: left hand moves and acts, right hand looks. The worst layout (a laptop without numpad) still works because no key is outside the main block.

HUDAlways minimal: the world is the interface.

Menus & feedback

  • Main menu (ENTER / L / Q), pause (ESC), phone (TAB), document reader ([E] Close).
  • Red vignette + "RUN!" in chases; subtitles for story lines ("3:17 AM. No signal.", "Don't look behind you."); screen-edge arrows; stamina bar.
  • Arabic uses full right-to-left layout and letter shaping with a fall-back that keeps Latin words and numbers readable.

Audio (procedural)

  • All sound is synthesised at runtime and cached as AudioClips: breath (calm and fast "fear breath"), heartbeat (soft and hard), footsteps, wet steps, drones, alarms, CRT hum, knocks, whispers and the patient's breath / skitter / hiss.
  • v1.0 additions: old rusty iron-door sounds (latch click, hinge squeal, heavy clang, slam, locked handle rattle) and a slide scrape.
  • Tension drives the mix: breathing and heartbeat rise, the drone pitches up, chase and tension music layers cross-fade; the player's own breathing is deliberately quiet so the Patient's is heard.

Technology, QA & Roadmap

Technology

ArchitectureBootstrap creates Audio, UI, PostFX, Level and Player; StoryDirector runs the stage coroutines and checkpoints; PatientAI + PatientBody; NavGraph for pathing; NoiseSystem for hearing; LightManager / LightFixture for power states; GameUI builds all UI in code.

Quality assurance

  • Autopilot regression: -autotest plays all 11 stages through the real player controls; the final build reached the Ending stage in ~410 s on every run with 0 exceptions in the player log.
  • Launcher smoke test from a clean unzip: game starts, responds, no errors.
  • Slide mechanic verified through the player log (start/end events); a human play-test of the final chase is still pending.

Known limitations

  • No in-game settings menu (volume, sensitivity, key rebinding).
  • Single ending; no difficulty setting; keyboard and mouse only.
  • Darkness is intentional but may be hard on some monitors - a brightness option is planned.
